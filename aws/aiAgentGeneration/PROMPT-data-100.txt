Analogy Library 

TOPIC: Gradient Descent 
Analogy: Walking downhill in fog so thick you can see nothing beyond your own boots. All you can sense is the slope of the ground directly underfoot. So you feel which way is downhill. 

TOPIC: Ridge regularization
Analogy: Restricting the theta parameter to a circle surrounding zero on the loss surface. The size of the circle is inversely correlated with lambda. 

TOPIC: OLS
Analogy: If we think about OLS in 2d, we can visualize Y as a line vector on top of a 2d plane representing the span of X. Then the Y-hat prediction becomes the shadow of the Y vector on the span of X, the part of X closest to it. We can visualize the residuals as a vector falling from the tip of Y to Y-hat. 

TOPIC: Cross-Validation
Analogy: Studying for an exam with a stack of practice problems. You cannot judge your readiness on problems you have already worked through with the solutions in front of you. So you set some aside untouched, study on the rest, then test yourself on the ones you held back — and rotate which ones you hold back, so no single unlucky selection decides your estimate.

TOPIC: K-Means Clustering
Analogy: A town is deciding where to put k post offices. Drop them anywhere to start. Every household then uses whichever office is closest, which carves the town into service areas. Once you see who each office actually serves, you move it to the middle of its own customers, and because it moved, some households on the edges are now closer to a different office, so you redraw the areas and move again. Repeat until nobody switches.

TOPIC: Hierarchical Clustering
Analogy: Merging villages into administrative districts. Start with every household in its own village. Find the two closest villages, merge them, and repeat until the whole region is one unit. The record of what merged with what, and how far apart things were at each merge, is the history you keep. The subtlety is what "closest" means once villages contain more than one household, the distance between the nearest two houses across the border, the farthest two, or the average. Those different rules produce different districts from the same map.

TOPIC: PCA 
Analogy: Casting a shadow of a physical object onto a wall. The object is three-dimensional and the shadow is flat, so you lose something no matter how you hold it, but not all angles lose the same amount. Held one way, a chair casts a shadow you can immediately recognize. Rotated ninety degrees, the same chair casts a narrow blob. PCA is the search for the orientation whose shadow is most spread out, then the one after that, and so on.

TOPIC: Logistic Regression
Analogy: A weather forecast and the decision to carry an umbrella. The forecaster's job ends at "70% chance of rain." Whether you take the umbrella is a separate decision, and it depends on things the forecast knows nothing about: how much you mind getting wet, whether you are carrying anything else, how far you are walking. Two people can hear the identical forecast and make opposite choices, and neither has disagreed with the forecaster.
